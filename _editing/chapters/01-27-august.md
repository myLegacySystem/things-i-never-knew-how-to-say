# 27 August

Here is a story.

Or maybe just a day that I don't want to forget.

It started on 26 August. She messaged me asking if I was free tomorrow.

Tomorrow was 27 August, a Thursday.

Honestly, I was supposed to be at work.

But instead of saying yes or no, I asked her, "Tell me first, why?"

She told me she wanted to go and see a college where she was thinking of taking admission, and enquire about the fee structure.

I asked her if it was possible to go on Friday instead, because Friday was a holiday for my company.

She said no. "It won't work. No problem, I'll go by myself."

And that one sentence immediately made me worried.

Would she be okay going alone?

Was it really a good idea to let her go by herself?

I don't know why I worried so much.

But I did.

So I told her I'd take a half day and come with her.

Surprisingly, she agreed.

And just like that, we decided to go together.

It wasn't a date.

It wasn't supposed to be anything like that.

She just needed to go to a college. And I was going with her.

But I was happy.

Because I was going to meet her.

I was going to spend some time with her.

And that was enough to make me excited.

The whole thing was happening tomorrow, but somehow I was already waiting for the morning.

Then the sun rose.

And I was already excited. I was ready before I was even supposed to be ready.

I reached the station fifteen minutes early and started waiting for her.

And then my brain started doing what it always does.

What will she be wearing?

How will she greet me?

What will her first reaction be when she sees me?

How am I going to react?

How should I greet her?

All these thoughts were running around in my head.

And as the time got closer, my heart started beating faster. I kept looking into the distance, waiting for my eyes to catch her.

But they didn't.

She was late.

I messaged her.

No reply.

A few more minutes passed. I started getting worried that maybe she wasn't coming.

Then, after about ten minutes, she called. She had left home. She was just a little late.

And for that moment, I felt relieved.

She hadn't cancelled.

She was coming.

So I went back to waiting.

And then I saw her from a distance.

She was wearing a pink top.

And coincidentally, I was wearing a pink shirt too.

I don't know why, but suddenly butterflies started flying around in my stomach.

I couldn't even say hi.

She walked toward the station without seeing me. And I just stood there.

I had seen her.

I could have called her name.

I could have said hi.

But I hesitated.

I don't even know why.

Maybe I was nervous.

Maybe I was just too happy to see her.

After she reached the platform, she called me. I told her I was near the ticket counter and that I hadn't seen her. We talked for a while, trying to figure out where we were going to meet and which train we should catch.

And after some time, we finally found each other.

She saw me and started laughing. We were both wearing almost the same colour.

Pink.

She found it funny.

I did too.

We talked for a little while before the train arrived. I don't remember everything we talked about.

I mostly remember us smiling.

And me blushing.

Mostly me.

When the train came, we got on and sat beside each other. We talked about random things.

She was smiling.

I was watching her.

But whenever she looked at me, I immediately looked away.

I couldn't keep eye contact with her.

I don't know how people do that.

Maybe that's what happens when your favourite person looks directly at you. You can spend hours wanting to look at them without blinking, but the moment they look back at you, suddenly you don't know where to look.

And of course, she is my favourite person.

The person I could probably look at all day if I wasn't so bad at looking into her eyes.

We eventually reached our destination. We got off the train and started looking for an auto. I was struggling to find one.

She was standing there under the sun, sweating, and I started getting worried about her. I wanted to find an auto quickly. Or at least find somewhere she could stand in the shade.

Finally, I found one.

We got in and started talking again.

At one point, her hair kept coming onto my face.

I gently moved it aside.

She laughed.

And somehow, even something that small made me happy.

We reached the college.

It was huge.

And honestly, while we were there, I kept thinking that this could be a really good place for her.

At the same time, another thought quietly appeared in the corner of my heart.

What if this college becomes a reason she goes away from me?

What if she makes new friends here?

What if her life changes?

I didn't say any of that.

I just kept listening to her.

She started telling me about the course she wanted to take. Her thoughts about the college. The admission process. Everything she had been thinking about.

I listened.

And I watched her while she talked.

I liked listening to her talk about her future.

Even if a small part of me was scared of where that future might take her.

After the enquiry was done, we left. We got into another auto. She continued talking about the course and the college, and I kept listening.

After some time, it started raining.

And not the soft kind. It was heavy.

We thought we'd have to open the umbrella. But before we really had to, the rain stopped.

By the time we got out of the auto, we were back at the station.

I said, "Let's eat something."

And we started looking for a café.

I was struggling to find the right one. She was just tagging along with me, watching me struggle to figure out where we should go.

I'm pretty sure she found it funny.

And while I was walking around, another thought came into my head.

Maybe I'm wasting her time.

Maybe I'm wasting her energy.

We're just walking around looking for a café.

But she never complained.

She just stayed with me.

She kept talking to me.

And I liked that.

She never made me feel like I was disappointing her, even when I was clearly failing at something as simple as finding a place to eat.

After a few minutes, we finally found a café near the station. There were very few people inside. It was quiet.

So we went in.

We ordered dosa. And before the food even arrived, we were already talking.

Then the dosa arrived.

I don't know why, but I was waiting for her to take the first bite. Then I was going to eat.

But she looked at me and said, "Are you going to eat now, or should I let you eat with my hands?"

I said yes.

And she put the dosa in my mouth.

I was so happy.

I did the same thing for her.

We were both blushing.

Both smiling.

And neither of us seemed to mind how silly we probably looked.

Then she did it again.

And I let her.

After that, we finished eating and went to get watermelon juice.

And somehow, we ended up sharing the same glass.

I was blushing again.

Even when I tried to stop, I couldn't.

I was just happy.

Really happy.

For a while, I forgot about everything else.

Work.

Time.

Everything.

Then reality started catching up.

I had to go to the office.

The time was getting close.

And honestly, I didn't want to leave.

I wanted that day to keep going.

I wanted to keep sitting there.

Keep talking.

Keep walking around with her.

Just a little longer.

But eventually, we got onto the bridge.

And I handed her the chocolate I had bought for her that morning.

She was happy.

I was happy.

And I still didn't want to leave.

But she pushed me and told me to go. Otherwise, I would be late for the office.

And just like that, the time was over.

That great time.

That little piece of the day that somehow became one of the best times I've ever had.

It was over.

But that's okay.

We had already talked about meeting again soon.

So while I was leaving, I was already thinking about the next time I'd see her.

I was still blushing.

Still smiling.

Still thinking about her.

And honestly, I think I was happy for the rest of the day just because of those few hours.

Maybe it wasn't a date.

Maybe it wasn't anything special to anyone else.

It was just going to a college.

Taking a train.

Finding an auto.

Walking around.

Eating dosa.

Sharing watermelon juice.

Giving her a chocolate.

And then going back to work.

But to me, it was much more than that.

It was a day with her.

And sometimes, that's enough.
